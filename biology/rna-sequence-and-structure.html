From RNA sequence to structure

Base pairing, folding, and the role of the molecular environment.

This sample demonstrates the article format for this blog.

Sequence permits multiple pairings

RNA bases can form complementary pairs, but a sequence may support several competing arrangements. Secondary structure describes pairing patterns; it does not fully specify the three-dimensional fold.

Context influences folding

Ions, temperature, interacting proteins, and other molecules can change the conformations an RNA adopts. A useful structural description therefore considers the experimental conditions and the possibility of multiple states.

Bring different measurements together

Chemical probing, structural experiments, and functional assays constrain different aspects of an RNA. Combining compatible evidence can narrow the possibilities, while disagreements can reveal missing assumptions or alternative states.
