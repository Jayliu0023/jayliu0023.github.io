How structure helps explain protein function

A short introduction to the relationship between a protein’s shape, interactions, and function.

This sample demonstrates the article format for this blog.

Structure provides a starting point

A protein’s three-dimensional structure helps us understand how it interacts with other molecules. A binding pocket, for example, can suggest where a small molecule might bind and which residues could contribute to recognition.

A structure is part of the evidence

Proteins can adopt multiple conformations. A single structure does not capture every state, and a structural observation alone does not prove a biological mechanism. Biochemical experiments and other measurements help test the interpretation.

A useful question to ask

When reading a structural biology paper, connect each proposed mechanism to the evidence that supports it: what was directly observed, what was inferred, and what remains to be tested?
