Reading a biology paper critically

A simple way to separate observations, interpretations, and open questions.

This sample demonstrates the article format for this blog.

Identify the question

Start by stating the biological question in one sentence. Then locate the experiments that address it directly, rather than treating every result in the paper as equally central to the conclusion.

Follow the controls

Ask what each control rules out and which alternative explanations remain. Consider replication, measurement uncertainty, and whether the experimental system supports the scope of the claim.

Keep conclusions proportional

Distinguish an association from a causal explanation and a plausible mechanism from a demonstrated one. A useful reading note records the main finding, the evidence supporting it, and a question that a follow-up experiment could test.
