Reading a cryo-EM map

What a reconstruction can tell us, and why local evidence matters.

This sample demonstrates the article format for this blog.

From particles to a reconstruction

Single-particle cryo-electron microscopy combines many noisy projection images to estimate a three-dimensional reconstruction. The resulting map provides evidence for molecular shape and, where sufficiently resolved, finer structural details.

Resolution varies across the map

A single reported resolution does not describe every region equally well. Flexible or heterogeneous regions may be less resolved than a stable core. Interpret a model against the local quality of the map rather than assuming every atom is equally supported.

Check the model against the evidence

A plausible model should fit the map while maintaining reasonable geometry. A close visual fit alone is not enough: independent validation and checks for clashes, unusual geometry, and unsupported regions help identify problems.
